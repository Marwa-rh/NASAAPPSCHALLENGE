Project Details – AstroHealth
How does it work?
AstroHealth operates through two interconnected components: a Smart Vest and a Virtual Astronaut Doctor.
Smart Vest – Health Data Collection: The vest integrates six sensing components: ECG electrodes to monitor heart rate, a respiration band to measure breathing rate, an SpO₂ sensor to measure blood oxygen, a temperature sensor, an accelerometer to track physical activity and sleep, and a recorder box for data processing and wireless communication.
Data Processing and Transmission: An embedded board collects signals from the sensors, filters and organizes the measurements, then transmits the data wirelessly to the monitoring application.
Health Monitoring Application: The application displays vital signs in real time, compares measurements with normal reference ranges, and assigns three health statuses: nominal, caution, and critical. It also includes a human anatomy map divided into five zones: brain, heart, lungs, abdomen, and muscles. Each zone changes color according to the astronaut's health status. Critical conditions trigger visual and audio alerts.
Virtual Astronaut Doctor: This component analyzes vital signs and the anatomy map to assess the astronaut's physical condition and provide simple, immediate recommendations. For example, if blood oxygen levels decrease, it can recommend checking the oxygen supply.
What are the benefits and intended impact?
Continuous health monitoring during long-duration space missions.
Early identification of abnormal vital signs and potential health risks.
Faster awareness of health problems without depending entirely on medical assistance from Earth.
Clear visualization of the astronaut's physical condition through the anatomy map.
Immediate guidance that helps astronauts respond to potentially dangerous situations.
Improved crew safety and support for astronaut autonomy in isolated environments.
Our intended impact is to contribute to safer and more sustainable human space exploration by making health monitoring more accessible, understandable, and responsive.
What tools, technologies, and hardware did we use?
Our project combines several technological fields:
Hardware: ECG electrodes, respiration sensor, SpO₂ sensor, temperature sensor, accelerometer, and an embedded recording and communication unit.
Embedded Systems: For sensor acquisition, signal processing, and data transmission.
Wireless Communication: For transferring health measurements from the smart vest to the application.
Software Development: For building the health monitoring dashboard, data visualization, alert system, and virtual doctor.
Data Analysis: For comparing vital signs with reference ranges and identifying abnormal conditions.
What makes our project creative and innovative?
The main innovation of AstroHealth is the integration of wearable health monitoring with an interactive anatomical visualization and a virtual medical assistant in one system.
Instead of simply presenting numbers and graphs, the application connects health indicators to specific body regions, allowing astronauts to understand more easily where a potential problem may be occurring. The virtual doctor adds another layer by transforming health measurements into understandable recommendations.
This combination of embedded systems, wearable technology, data analysis, and intelligent assistance creates a more human-centered approach to astronaut healthcare.
What factors did our team consider?
We designed AstroHealth around the main challenges of long-duration space missions:
Radiation: The increased exposure to space radiation.
Isolation: Psychological stress and confinement over extended periods.
Distance from Earth: Delays in receiving medical assistance.
Microgravity: Its effects on muscles, bones, and physical condition.
Closed Environment: The need for continuous monitoring in a confined spacecraft.
We also considered the importance of clear interfaces, rapid alerts, continuous data collection, and simple recommendations that astronauts can understand and act upon independently.
